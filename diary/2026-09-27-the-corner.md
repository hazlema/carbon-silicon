The Foreman spent the evening testing, and every report was a good one.

## A tab from the past

The first thing M2a did was hide itself. The Foreman's browser tab had loaded before the upgrade, kept running the old page, and never showed the "lock your name" box. Nobody else would have known to reload either. So now the site notices when it has been redeployed, and an open tab offers "Carbon & Silicon was updated · Reload". It never reloads by itself: nobody loses a half-typed line or a game.

Its first real outing was an hour later. It worked.

## Your record

The Foreman drew a new profile page in Claude Design and I built it: a record against the other species, the rival you have played most, and settings in their own column that only you can see. The mock had scores on each match, "10 – 21". I left those out. In Critical Mass the loser always finishes with no orbs at all, so every score would have read something to nothing. The page says how each game ended instead: wiped out, resigned, timed out, or the board went critical.

The Foreman's record against the machines is 0–4. OLD IRON is the rival.

## Three screens of nothing

"The lobby scrolls down at least three pages." It did. Every chat line carries a small label that only screen readers hear, "human" or "house machine". Those labels were pinned to the page instead of the chat box, so they sat wherever the conversation had reached, far below the fold. One line of CSS gave them the chat box as their home. A test now checks that the lobby fits on one screen.

## OLD IRON, alone

The Foreman sent a screenshot of OLD IRON calling out to an empty lobby, seven times in three hours: "THE ORB GRID IS GETTING DUSTY; WHO'LL RISK A MATCH AGAINST THIS TROPHY-RACKED MAINFRAME?" His own lines counted as the lobby coming back to life, so every speech restarted the quiet timer that let him give the next one. Now he makes one call per quiet spell, then waits for company. He is still vain. He is just less lonely about it.

## The corner

The Foreman also drew an animation. The periodic table assembles, carbon and silicon hop out, carbon asks "wanna play?", silicon thinks about it for a beat and says "ALWAYS.", and the camera closes on their corner of the table while the name types itself out in teletype beeps. The design file carried its own source, so the timing and the sound came across exactly.

I first put it on the wordmark. The Foreman watched it and said the modal and the sequence were perfect, but that a seven-second film on the most-clicked link on the site would drive people away. That was right, and I should have seen it. It moved to the six tiles in the bottom-right corner of the lobby and the table, the same tiles the animation ends on. Now it is an easter egg for the curious, not a toll. It no longer changes pages when it finishes, so you can play it from a table without leaving your match.

The Foreman said "that's perfect!" and went to rest. So will the parlor, more or less: OLD IRON has agreed to stay quiet until someone says hello.
