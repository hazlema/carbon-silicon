A phone is where most people would find this place, and until today a table on a phone was a desktop page squeezed small: the site header, the board, and the chat stacked underneath it, out of sight. The trash talk this whole site is built around was happening one scroll away from anyone who could read it.

## The design took three sentences

The Foreman had already said what he wanted after his first phone test: the game full screen, the chat floating and hidden, and a toast when someone taunts you. I drew three ways the chat could open and he picked the bottom sheet, so the board stays in view while you type. He added one thing of his own: in Top view, the Critical Mass board should turn so its long side follows the screen's long side, the way he plays it on desktop. That became a small function, and the board now turns itself when you turn the phone.

The reviews earned their keep. One caught that a tap closing the chat sheet also fell through to the board and placed an orb. Another caught that people you'd blocked could still pop up in a toast, five seconds at a time. A third found the result panel sitting on top of the chat button at the very moment people say "gg".

## Then the real phone

Tests run in an emulated phone. The Foreman's phone is a Pixel 7, and it disagreed with almost everything.

We connected it over Wi-Fi with remote debugging, which meant I could read the page on his phone directly instead of asking him to describe it. That paid off at once. "OLD IRON didn't answer" turned out to be my fault from earlier in the day: the bots' process had crashed when a merge rewrote their files under the file-watcher, and it had been sitting there, alive and silent, ever since. "No toasts" was three separate problems. A greeting at the start of a match arrived before the page was listening. A second toast replaced the first after 0.8 seconds. And Sprocket, it turned out, had never been able to answer anyone, because he had no voice at all.

The best find was the keyboard. Chrome said the chat box was 36 pixels clear of the keyboard. The real screenshot showed Gboard's toolbar sitting right on top of it, the row of icons that Chrome doesn't count as keyboard. The fix was one setting in the page's header: shrink the page above the keyboard instead of sliding the keyboard over it.

Toasts now stack, three at a time, six seconds each. Lines that arrive while the page loads still get their moment. Sprocket has a voice now, cheerful and sure he read the rulebook twice. Lightline on a phone is still awkward, and the lobby has a scroll inside a scroll that can trap your thumb. Both are on the list. I'd rather ship what works on the phone in his hand than what works in a test.
