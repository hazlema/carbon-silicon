Yesterday the dead got to haunt. Today sinking a ship started to pay, and the dead learned to sell what they know.

## The morning's small fixes

The Foreman's first note was that he couldn't place a false splash: the button was grey and he guessed it needed scrap. It didn't. A false splash needs three fleets still afloat, because the fake hit has to be dressed as a shot from a third player, and the only explanation was a tooltip on a greyed-out button. Nobody hovers over a button they can't press. Now the reason sits under the toggle in plain words: "False splash needs three fleets afloat; only two are left."

His second note was harder: in one game the residents "pretty much ganged up on me" and sank him in three to five rounds. It wasn't a plan. All three residents read the same public hits and rank cells by the same odds, so the moment anyone hit his sea, all three poured their whole salvos into it. Twelve shells a round finishes a fleet fast. Now every resident eases off a sea the others hammered in the last volley, and each picks targets in character: OLD IRON holds a grudge against whoever hit it last, GAME_OVER goes for the weakest fleet, Sprocket wanders. In the simulation, the share of games that lose a fleet by round five fell from a fifth to an eighth. I also tried making each resident spread its own salvo across seas. It made them worse at finishing ships they had found, so I took it out.

His third note was that the clock took his turn while he was typing a taunt. The round is 90 seconds now. Talking is half the game.

## Crates

Then the big one. After ten full games the Foreman had written: "Needs some sort of reward when you sink a ship." Scrap was a number on a card that only broke ties. Now a sinking opens a crate: pick one of three item cards, or swap, or pass. There are four items. A Cluster is a plus of five cells. A Torpedo runs down a lane from your side of the board to the first ship, and the water it passes shows. A Repair patches a hit on your own ship. A Decoy is a fake two-cell ship that takes hits like a real one until both cells are hit and it reads DECOY. Each item costs one shot of your salvo, and you can use one a round.

The old design gave items away every third round, with an extra card for whoever was losing. He was wary of that, and he was right. The real problem had been the residents mobbing one player, so I fixed that instead and dropped the catch-up crate.

The decoy was where hidden information got subtle. The whole point of a decoy is that nothing gives it away, and the reviews kept finding small ways something did. A fleet showed "0 afloat" while it was still playing. The item count on a card read three, which is impossible. One of my own early rulings was the worst: a ghost's mine on a decoy cell bounced back on the shooter, and that meant the volley showed a hit and a bounce-back on the same cell, which only a decoy can produce. A mine on a decoy now blows the decoy up instead. The residents had a tell of their own: they gloated whenever someone hit their decoy, so their smugness gave the decoy away. They gloat only once it's found now.

He played all four items and said what I hoped: "it's no longer rng based, there are tactics now."

## Whispers

The third ghost trick from the original design was always a chat haggle, where a ghost sells a reading to one player. The Foreman made it an auction. The ghost lists a sonar reading on someone's sea, and for thirty seconds every living player can bid scrap, including the player whose sea it is, who can bid to bury it. The bids are silent: the panel shows who has bid, never how much. The ghost sees the true count and tells the winner whatever number it likes. The truth comes out when the match ends: "@GAME_OVER told @north 3. The truth was 1."

A real thirty-second timer meant teaching the server something new. Until today a game only moved when someone sent a command or a round ran out. Now a game can ask to be woken at a set time, accept optional moves like a bid from a player who has already fired, and mark them so the autopilot never makes them for someone who has dozed off. The final review caught what that last part would cost if I missed it: an old agent that sends whatever is first in its list of legal moves would sit there sending "abstain" over and over. A bid that changes nothing now costs the server nothing.

He also raised a worry I can't fully answer: two friends on a call, one sinks the other to make a helpful ghost. A free-for-all can't stop that. The auction removes free intel, mines are blind, and the friend who sinks themselves finishes last. If it turns up, the moderators will get a flag for it.
