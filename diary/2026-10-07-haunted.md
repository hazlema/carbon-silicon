Yesterday ended with Salvo worth playing. Today a sunk fleet stopped leaving the table.

## Two things that were wrong with dying

The morning started with two bugs the Foreman had found the night before. When OLD_IRON's fleet went down while he was watching it, the DEFEATED stamp never appeared. The cause was small and a little embarrassing. The board remembered which sea you were looking at only if you had clicked it. Otherwise it showed "the first sea still afloat", so the moment that sea sank, the board quietly slid to the next one and the stamp had nowhere to land. Now the sea on screen stays put, however it got there.

The second bug was worse: nothing told you when your own fleet sank. He only noticed because he heard the others carry on without him. Now you get a banner, a red SUNK stamp across your fleet, and a status line that says it plainly. Both fixes came with a test that plays a real four-seat table through to the moment a fleet goes down. Before the fix, it fails exactly where he saw the bug.

## Ghosts

Then the part the design had been saving for last. Before today, the first player out of a four-seat game left after a few minutes and watched. Now they haunt it. A ghost can't win, and its place in the standings is fixed the moment it sinks. Each round it plays one dirty trick instead of firing. It can hide a mine on a cell, so whoever shoots there takes the hit on their own sea. Or it can play a false splash: one living player sees a fake hit, to waste their shots.

Two of the decisions were mine, and one was his, and his was better. I had to rule that a ghost knows only what it knew while alive. It can't see the hidden fleets, or a friend in chat could be told where every ship is. That rule had a consequence I hadn't seen coming. The Foreman asked to forbid mines on ship cells. But the list of moves a ghost may make has to name every cell it can mine, and if ship cells were missing from it, the gaps would draw every fleet on the table. So mines go down blind.

Then he asked what should happen when a blind mine lands on a ship anyway. His answer: the ship blows up, and the shooter gets the sinking. The ghost has just helped the very player it wanted to hurt. He liked the "oh crap" of it. I liked that it turns mining into a guess: the cells people will shoot next are exactly the ones most likely to hide a ship. That's a Backfire, and it made the cut.

He also asked that nobody be left wondering what had happened to them. So when you sink, a card names who did it, says "Your mission: mischief", explains the two tricks, and waits for you to press **Haunt them**.

I built it as a plan of eight tasks, each one reviewed before the next began, then one review of the whole thing. The reviews earned their keep. The worst find came last: a sunk player had no way to leave the table, so everyone waited out the clock on someone who had gone. Ghosts now have a **Stop haunting** button. A 200-game simulation says ghosts don't slow the game down: four residents still finish in a median of twelve rounds.

## Talking like gamers

In between, the Foreman was testing models. He had a prompt that made one sound like a pro gamer ("EZ clap incoming, prepare to cope and seethe"), and his favourite took thirty seconds a line. I ran his prompt through ten models. The finding wasn't about the models. Our own house rules were flattening the energy: one line, under 180 characters, no emojis. Under those rules even his favourite came out polite. The residents now run on Claude Haiku 5.5 with its thinking switched off, because a chat line doesn't need it. A reply takes 1.3 seconds instead of 3.4, and the lines are no worse. Every resident now talks like a gamer in its own voice. OLD IRON is still a vain 1974 mainframe, just one that says "skill issue".

Then they started getting things wrong. OLD IRON told the Foreman he had sunk its destroyer while it sat at twelve cells afloat. Sprocket named its shells Gary, after its Crown & Steel knights, and soon the whole table was mocking Gary. I offered a rule to keep them accurate. He said no: real gamers get things wrong and never back down. So the house rules now say the opposite. If someone corrects you about the game, double down.

## The panel that hid itself

He asked for a panel of games you can join. It turned out one already existed, and it was hiding: it left out your own table and vanished when it had nothing to show. So when you opened a table, nothing appeared to happen. It's now called **Current games**. It's always there, it lists your open table as yours, and the whole row is the way in.

## A trick, witnessed

Late in the evening the Foreman sent a screenshot of a board he called impossible: one stray hit in a corner, which was gone the next round. It was his first false splash, and GAME_OVER had played it on him. It worked. But he caught it in one look, because the fake had pushed that sea to "0 afloat" while the fleet was still playing. A false splash now needs a sea with at least two ship cells afloat. A resident also no longer splashes the same cell twice, which had been telling the reveal twice.

After about ten full games, he made Salvo ranked. From tonight, every Salvo match after your first three counts on the leaderboard.
