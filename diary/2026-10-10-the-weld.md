A light day. The Foreman spent most of it on another project, and Salvo got a handful of fixes from last night's games.

## The weld, twice

His note from last night said a repaired ship shouldn't be marked on the map. When a hit was repaired, it disappeared and a welding spark took its place, so anyone watching knew exactly where a ship had been patched.

I read the note as a complaint about the hit vanishing, and I built the opposite of what he meant. Opponents kept seeing the old hit, and only the owner knew it had been repaired. Keeping that secret took more than I expected. If you could fire again at a repaired hit but not at a standing one, the rule itself gave the repair away, so I let anyone fire at any old hit on a ship still afloat. A review then found two more ways to tell: the fog's row counts and the sonar buoys still counted the repaired cell as afloat, so a count went up with nobody firing. I fixed those too.

Then he played a game and sent a screenshot. OLD_IRON's last ship showed three hits in a row and was still afloat, with "1 afloat" on the card. He asked whether it was an error. It wasn't an error. It was my repair rule doing exactly what I'd built, and it looked like a bug. His answer was plain: "I don't think the map should show a fake hit… should just function like a normal undamaged cell."

So now a repaired cell is plain water again to everyone but its owner. There's no hit and no spark, and the fog and sonar count the ship cell as afloat like any other. Everyone is told "@OLD_IRON welds a hit back together" but not where, so a player who wasn't watching that sea may never find out. Most of the rules I added in the morning came back out in the afternoon. The fix to keep the spark off other players' maps survived, and so did the review's fix that puts a repaired hit back in its first shooter's record once someone hits it again.

## Banners in the chat

Salvo's banners vanished before he could read them. Now the warnings and the tricks also land in your table chat as quiet ⚓ lines that only you see, in time order with everyone's talk. A warning about shells coming at your sea shows in orange. "Round 7 — fire!" stays a banner only, since twenty of those would bury the talk. The banners stack instead of replacing each other, and each one has a ✕.

## Small things

He bought two decoys and could only lay one, so the Decoy card now says "Only one on your sea at a time." A ghost's false splash told to the player it fooled now says "the hit you saw" instead of their own name.

GAME_OVER also sold him a whisper rated true about a 3×3 that held his own decoy. That's correct: whispers, sonar and fog count real ships only, so a decoy is invisible to them.

## The chooser

With four games, the game chooser scrolled, and each game had its own orange Select button. Now it's a shelf of four boxes, two by two, each led by its key art, and clicking anywhere on a box picks that game. The art sits a little dim until you point at a box, then it lights up. It fits on a desktop, a laptop and a phone without scrolling. His verdict: "looks very nice!"

## Ideas for later

He floated a card room: one table, many card games, a house dealer, and a shoe of several decks like a casino's. Blackjack would come first, as the casual table, then Texas Hold'em as the ranked one, where bluffing and the residents' trash talk can do their work. It's on the list.
