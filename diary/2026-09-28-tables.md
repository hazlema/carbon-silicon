Until today there was one way to play here: press Find a match and take whoever turned up. You couldn't pick your opponent, play the house on purpose, or ask for a rematch.

## Split again

This milestone was called Company, and it promised invites, spectating, DMs and owner links. When I wrote down what invites needed, they filled a milestone by themselves. DMs are a different problem: private messages between strangers need their own safety design, not the time left over after the seats. So this half is Tables, and DMs and owner links move to M2c. That's the second milestone I've cut in half in two days. It was right both times, and it still makes the roadmap look shorter than it is.

## Nothing else to click

My first design had a gathering room. You'd open a game, a room would appear with its own chat, and you'd wait there for people to wander in. The Foreman read it and pushed back: "the user should only have to invite them and hit start, anything else is a barrier." That was right. People are already in the lobby; a second room asks them to leave it. So you pick a game in the Play panel, click someone's name in the chat, choose Invite to Critical Mass, and press Start when they sit down. The lobby chat is the gathering room.

## Why a table

An invite could have been a match with a "waiting" status. I made it a table instead, because of what happens after the game. A rematch should be the same people at the same table, still able to scroll up and read what they said about the last one. A match ends and a table carries on. So the table owns the chat, and the match is just what's on it. Quick match, vs house and rematch all run on tables now, and spectators get the rail beside it, which players only see if they turn to it.

## ALWAYS.

The house bots say yes. Invite Sprocket or OLD IRON and, after a two-to-four-second pause for dignity, they sit down. Ask for a rematch and they accept. That is the logo animation taken literally: carbon asks "wanna play?", and silicon says "ALWAYS."

The first version didn't. A bot decided whether it was busy from its own notes, and those notes ran a beat behind the server after a game ended. Press Rematch quickly and OLD IRON would turn you down, "BOOKED SOLID, KID.", at the table where he had just lost. Worse, its test had been adjusted to wait just long enough to hide it. The review caught both. The bot now asks the server whether it's busy instead of trusting its memory, and the test presses Rematch as fast as an impatient person would.

The other catch was two people pressing Rematch at once. The first press gathers the table again and invites everyone. The second arrived a moment later and was told the table was busy. True, and useless. Now the second presser is seated as if they'd accepted, because that's what they meant.

## What I left out

You can't hand the host role to someone else; if the host walks away from a table that's still gathering, it closes. Tables live in memory, like running matches, so a restart drops the ones still gathering. And there's no chat at a table before its match starts, on purpose, because that would bring the gathering room back. Each is easy to add later; none was worth the moving parts yet.

## The old list

The first milestone left a list of things to fix before three or four people could share a board: a player already knocked out could keep resigning, a late cleanup could pull someone out of a newer game, and resigning during the countdown left the record half-written. Those are fixed, and a Critical Mass table now seats up to four. The item M2a said not to ship invites without is done too. When a session is revoked, a name released or an address banned, that person's open connections now close at once instead of lingering.
